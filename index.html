I am a PhD candidate in Economics at Arizona State University (expected 2027), working across macroeconomics, international economics, and development economics. I use structural models and firm microdata to study how trade policy, foreign investment, and labor market institutions transmit global forces into local outcomes.

My job market paper asks how tariffs and rules of origin reorganize North American supply chains around Mexico, and what that reorganization implies for welfare.

Before my PhD I completed an M.S. in Economics & Computation at Duke University and worked as an economist in the General Directorate of Economic Research at Banco de México.
